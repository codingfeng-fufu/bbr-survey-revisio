\subsection{Conditioned cross-version comparison}
The baseline, BBR2, and BBRv3 runs were analyzed with the same condition
keys, statistical window, and five-run repetition rule. The Ubuntu in-tree
baseline is retained as an implementation-specific reference and is not
renamed BBRv1. BBR2 and BBRv3 are identified by their booted kernel and
source markers; their runtime registration names are \texttt{bbr2} and \texttt{bbr},
respectively.

Across the tested 100-Mbps matrix, equal-RTT total goodput remains close to
the bottleneck for all three implementations, while flow shares and Jain
fairness vary with RTT asymmetry, queue setting, and implementation. Under
the 40- and 80-ms second-flow conditions, the observed long-RTT share and
queue-drop counts differ across implementations. These results support a
conditional comparison over the tested matrix; they do not establish a
universal version ranking or identify unmeasured internal state.

For the no-ECN BBR/CUBIC comparison, total goodput is similar across the
three implementation environments, while the BBR/CUBIC share and Jain
index remain condition dependent. The corresponding values and raw inputs
are archived in \texttt{cross\_version\_comparison.csv}; figures are generated by
\texttt{analyze\_cross\_version.py}.
