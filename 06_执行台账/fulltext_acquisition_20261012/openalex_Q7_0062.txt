Performance Evaluation of Trafﬁc Steering
Methods for QoS Improvement in case of
Network Failures in Beyond 5G Networks

Koki Kawaguchi, Daiki Nobayashi, Kazuya Tsukamoto, Takeshi Ikenaga




Abstract Mobile communication networks are expanding. However, when natu-
ral disasters happen and communication equipment is damaged, service disruptions
can occur and many people cannot use the network. When such disasters happen,
it is possible to use long-distance wireless networks such as Low Power Wide Area
(LPWA) networks or satellite communication networks as alternative routes. This
study focuses on the network between the Beyond 5G core network and the remote
host and proposes a resilient network that uses the open RAN (O-RAN) architec-
ture to utilize available communication networks during failures. Additionally, we
propose a trafﬁc steering method based on the types of user devices to effectively
use available alternative routes and alleviate QoS degradation for users. This paper
evaluates the effectiveness of the proposed system through simulations. The sim-
ulation results showed that the proposed methods increase the satisfaction rate of
ﬂows’ requirements.

1 Introduction

As 5G networks expand, mobile networks are becoming indispensable. However,
there are a lot of natural disasters such as earthquakes, typhoons, and heavy rains
in Japan. These natural disasters can damage communication equipment and cause
cable breaks, leading to communication disruptions. For example, the 2011 Great
East Japan Earthquake caused severe damage to NTT East’s communication infras-
tructure, resulting in widespread service outages across many regions[1]. During
such disasters, it is possible to use long-distance wireless networks such as LPWA
networks or satellite communication networks as alternative routes. For example, K.
Ali et al. [2] examined the utilization of emerging communication technologies such
as IoT, device-to-device communications, and cloud computing to maintain commu-
nication during disasters. There is a demand for networks that remain connected or
at least can quickly recover connections during disasters. Therefore, this study aims
to maintain network connectivity and alleviate the degradation of Quality of Service
(QoS) in the event of communication failures caused by natural disasters.
    This study proposes a resilient network for Beyond 5G network that uses the open
RAN (O-RAN) architecture [3] to leverage available communication networks dur-
ing failures. Additionally, we propose a trafﬁc steering method to effectively use
ﬁnite communication resources and alleviate the QoS degradation for users. This
study evaluates the effectiveness of the proposed resilient network system and the
ﬂow allocation method for User Equipment (UE). We compare the ﬂow allocation
method to a round-robin allocation of ﬂows from each UE to available wireless com-
munication networks. Using the network simulator ns-3, this study demonstrates
that the proposed method increases the satisfaction rate of user applications that
meet their required QoS.
    The signiﬁcance of this research lies in its potential to enhance the resilience and
reliability of future mobile networks. By addressing the critical issue of maintain-
ing communication during natural disasters, this study contributes to the ongoing
efforts to develop more robust and adaptive network infrastructures. Moreover, the
integration of multiple communication technologies and the use of advanced trafﬁc
steering algorithms represent a forward-thinking approach to network management
in the Beyond 5G era.
    The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews related studies on
trafﬁc steering in 5G networks and resilient network for natural disasters. Section
3 explains the details of the proposed method. Section 4 describes the simulation
environment constructed for the evaluation. Section 5 provides the evaluation results
and discussion. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper.



2 Related Works

This section introduces related research on trafﬁc steering within the O-RAN archi-
tecture and resilient networks for natural disasters.
    F. Kavehmadavani et al. [4] proposed a framework called JIFDR, which inte-
grates trafﬁc prediction, ﬂow-split, dynamic user association, and radio resource
management to address unknown dynamic trafﬁc demands within the O-RAN ar-
chitecture. Both F. Kavehmadavani et al. and this study leverage the O-RAN ar-
chitecture to manage dynamic trafﬁc demands. However, there are differences as
follows. F. Kavehmadavani et al. used a long-short-term memory (LSTM) model
for trafﬁc prediction and steering. This study focuses on trafﬁc steering based on the
applications used in UE.
    Next, we examine related studies on trafﬁc steering between 3GPP access net-
works such as 4G and 5G, and non-3GPP access networks such as Wi-Fi. T. Cogalan
et al. [5] proposed a method that measures the real-time link status and ofﬂoads data
to non-3GPP networks to reduce the network load. Both T. Cogalan et al. and this
study use multiple wireless networks for trafﬁc steering. However, there are differ-
ences as follows. T. Cogalan et al. used simultaneously multiple access networks to
send data. This study allocates ﬂows to a single route.
    Additionally, K. Ali et al. [2] discussed the importance of IoT and 5G networks
during natural disasters. The paper examined how to utilize emerging communi-
cation technologies such as IoT, device-to-device communications, vehicular net-
works, cloud and fog computing, unmanned aerial vehicles, and sensor networks for
communication during disasters. Both K. Ali et al. and this study propose resilient
communication networks for natural disasters. However, there are differences as fol-
lows. K. Ali et al. focused on a broader range of technologies and mainly addresses
the access network. In contrast, this study speciﬁcally targets the network between
the Beyond 5G core network and the remote host, proposing a resilient network
system using the O-RAN architecture and a trafﬁc steering method based on user
device applications.



3 Proposed Method

This study proposes a resilient network system that utilizes O-RAN and network
slicing within Beyond 5G networks. The system is designed to ﬂexibly use available
communication routes during failures and effectively manage ﬁnite communication
resources to alleviate QoS degradation for users. In this section, we introduce the
details of the proposed system and the trafﬁc steering method.

framework. This system assumes the use of LoRa for LPWA. The proposed system
allows the construction of a network that can ﬂexibly use multiple communication
paths. Under normal conditions, the system uses the Beyond 5G network, which
provides the largest bandwidth. The following sections describe the operation of the
system under normal conditions.
1. The non-real-time RAN intelligent controller (Non-RT RIC) stores the required
   throughput and latency for the applications used by each UE.
2. The Non-RT RIC also stores the bandwidth and latency data of wireless com-
   munication networks such as LEO satellite services, GEO satellite services, and
   LPWAN.
When a natural disaster damages communication equipment and the Beyond 5G
network becomes unavailable, the proposed system maintains UE communication
by using other available routes. However, these alternative routes may not provide
enough bandwidth and latency as well as the Beyond 5G network. Therefore, it is
necessary to allocate UE ﬂows appropriately to the ﬁnite communication resources.
The proposed system mitigates QoS degradation by allocating each ﬂow based on
the throughput and latency requirements of the applications used by the UEs. The
system operates in the following steps and allocates ﬂows for each UE to the alter-
native route:
1. The Non-RT RIC detects a communication failure.
2. When a UE connects to the 5G network, the Non-RT RIC sends the required
   throughput and latency for the applications used by the UE to the near-real-time
   RIC (Near-RT RIC). Additionally, the Non-RT RIC provides the bandwidth and
   latency of available wireless communication resources to the Near-RT RIC.
3. The Near-RT RIC determines the routing path for the ﬂow and notiﬁes the O-
   RAN Central Unit (O-CU) of the path.
4. The O-CU identiﬁes the packets based on the UE’s IP address and directs them
   to the speciﬁed route.

3.2 Trafﬁc Steering Method

This section describes the ﬂow allocation algorithm used by the system explained
in 3.1. The three alternative routes to the Beyond 5G network considered are LEO
satellite services, GEO satellite services, and LPWAN. The allocation of UE ﬂows
is based on the required throughput and latency of the applications used by the UEs.
The algorithm shown in Fig. 2 determines the path for each ﬂow. Note that A and B
in Fig. 2 represent either LEO satellite services or GEO satellite services.
    When a new UE connects to the 5G network, the system calculates the total re-
quired throughput by summing the required throughput of the ﬂows currently allo-
cated to LPWAN and the new UE’s required throughput. If the total does not exceed
LPWAN’s bandwidth and the required latency is longer than LPWAN’s latency, the
ﬂow is assigned to LPWAN. For the remaining ﬂows, they are ﬁrst categorized by
latency and then by throughput. If the sum of the required throughput values of
the ﬂows assigned to a path exceeds the path’s bandwidth, the system checks the
condition of the other path and makes the ﬁnal allocation accordingly.

5 Simulation Results

We simulated three times for both the proposed method and the comparison method.
Figure 3 shows the simulation results for the satisfaction rate of ﬂows that met the
required throughput and latency for each application used by the UEs. Additionally,
Figure 4a shows the average latency for ﬂows requiring shorter latency, and Figure
4b shows the average latency for ﬂows without speciﬁc latency requirements.
   Compared to the comparison method, the proposed method increased the satis-
faction rate of ﬂows meeting the required throughput by 15.1 points and the required
latency by 30.3 points. The uneven distribution of data across the ﬂows led to an 87.4
% satisfaction rate for the required throughput, rather than a uniform reduction in
throughput across all ﬂows. The 85.2 % satisfaction rate for the required latency
resulted from some ﬂows being assigned to GEO satellite paths. This occurred be-
cause the LEO satellite paths reached their capacity, despite those ﬂows requiring
shorter latency.
   Additionally, the proposed method achieved a shorter average latency for ﬂows
requiring low latency. For example, in the case of Video Streaming I, the average
latency decreased from 420.3 ms to 382.9 ms with the proposed method. In con-
trast, the latency increased for File uploads. The comparison method allocated the
path to either LEO satellite communication or GEO satellite route, while the pro-

6 Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a resilient network system that ﬂexibly leverages avail-
able communication networks during network failures. Additionally, we propose a
trafﬁc steering method to efﬁciently use ﬁnite communication resources and allevi-
ate QoS degradation for users. We simulated to verify the effectiveness of the pro-
posed method. Simulation results showed that the proposed method increased the
satisfaction rate of ﬂows meeting throughput and latency requirements compared to
the round-robin algorithm by steering trafﬁc based on application needs.
   A key future challenge is addressing scalability issues caused by different net-
work environments and an increasing number of users. We plan to conduct further
performance evaluations in real operational environments and tests with more di-
verse scenarios.
