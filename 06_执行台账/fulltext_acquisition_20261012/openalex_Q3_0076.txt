                               Abstract
 Google scientists published Bottleneck Bandwidth and Round-trip prop-
 agation time (BBR), a novel TCP congestion control algorithm variant.
 Unlike traditional congestion control algorithms, BBR adjusts its trans-
 mission rates by periodically measuring the network traffic based on
 round-trip propagation time (RTprop) and available bottleneck band-
 width (Btlbw). This results in BBR achieving higher delivery rates and
 minimizing the latency on our modern-day network infrastructure. How-
 ever, some recent experimental evaluations and analyses have shown
 severe RTT (round-trip time) fairness concerns in the TCP-BBR algo-
 rithm. When two competing flows with different RTTs coexist on a
 shared bottleneck link, BBR tends to prefer and favor longer RTT flows,

     therefore consuming more bandwidth share than short RTT counter-
     parts, which can result in short RTT flows being starved even to death.
     This preference nature of the BBR in favor of long RTT flows results in
     an acute throughput imbalance and an extreme BBR and network vul-
     nerability that malevolent individuals can easily exploit by increasing the
     delay (RTTs) in their flows to create unwarranted long RTTs on their
     TCP flows to unfairly obtain and enjoy the lion’s share of the available
     bandwidth than the rest of users on the network. The BBR-With Adap-
     tive Pacing Gain Rates (BBR-APG) algorithm is proposed in this study
     as an alternative solution to the TCP-BBR’s RTT fairness problem. Our
     proposed approach adaptively adjusts the pacing gain rates of each TCP
     flow based on the bottleneck inflight queue status during the bandwidth
     estimation phase instead of the default fixed pacing gain rates as provided
     by the original version of the BBR algorithm, which only works perfectly
     when only a single flow is involved. With the BBR-APG algorithm, differ-
     ent RTT flows can fairly compete for the available bottleneck bandwidth,
     thus enhancing the RTT fairness issue that has been compromised in
     the original version of the BBR algorithm. Through our extensive sim-
     ulation analysis on NS3, we identified that our proposed methodology
     improves RTT fairness by more than 18%, a reduction of more than 24%
     and 20% on packets retransmission rates and queuing delay, respectively,
     compared to other recently published BBR variants like an adaptive
     congestion window of BBR (BBR-ACW) on various network conditions.

1 Introduction
Despite the research community effort towards diminishing packet loss prob-
lems during data transmission, our networks still experience massive packet
loss, notably wireless networks [1, 2]. Packet losses may occur due to bottle-
neck buffer overflow (bufferbloat), network congestion, or network failures. In
the mid-1980s, the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) established network
congestion control which considers packet loss as the only sign of a congested
network, which is not always accurate in our modern networks [3]. TCP man-
ages the congestion window (cwnd) from the server (sender), intending to
control and govern the packets to be transmitted. Whenever no packet loss is
noticed or detected, the cwnd surges exponentially until congestion is detected
through duplicate acknowledgments (ACKs), and cwnd will be reduced by half
to slow down data transmission [4, 5]. The majority of the current and existing
TCP congestion control algorithms are loss-based, which read each packet loss
as a sign of a congested network and cause bottleneck bufferbloat by keeping
the bottleneck buffer always filled up [6, 7].
    Google’s research team published a novel TCP congestion control variant,
Bottleneck Bandwidth and Round-trip propagation time (BBR), as a better
alternative to classical loss-based congestion control algorithms. Contrary to

traditional TCP congestion control variants, BBR regularly measures Btlbw
and RTprop of the network traffic and utilizes the measured results to adjust
its transmission pattern. Therefore recording higher throughput with minimum
transmission delay [8]. In addition, BBR uses bandwidth-delay product (BDP)
estimates to reduce bufferbloat occurrences rather than applying a notation
of packet loss as an indicator of a congested network. As a result, BBR has a
significant comparative advantage over other TCP loss-based congestion con-
trol variants, especially in a lossy network setup. [9, 10]. BBR algorithm finds
the optimal operating point (Kleinrock’s optimal operating point) by measur-
ing the highest possible delivery rate with a very minimal delay time [11], as
demonstrated in Figure 1. Upon finding the optimal operating point, the BBR
delivery rate remains constant while RTT starts increasing as a sign that the
inflight packets are filling up the bottleneck buffer and the queue is starting to
pile up. BBR has shown significant throughput and delay improvement com-
pared to its predecessor algorithms [12, 13] such as Cubic [5] when implemented
on various Google in-house servers and B4 network [9].

• Startup
    The data-sending rate is increased by BBR using a pacing gain value rate
of 2/ln(2) until the actual bandwidth is identified. If the probing growth stops,
BBR leaves the startup phase and moves into the drain phase, assuming the
desired bandwidth has been reached.
• Drain
BBR applies an inverted startup pacing gain rate to reduce excessive data
transmitted during the previous phase. When the inflight data becomes 1 BDP,
BBR exits from this phase and enters probeBw.
• ProbeBw
    The majority of BBR’s time is spent on this phase. During this time, BBR
is driven by its delivery capacity estimates. The estimated delivery capacity is
then passed through the maximum bandwidth filter algorithm, which outputs
the available bandwidth estimation. BBR applies a pacing rate of 1.25 times
the measured bottleneck bandwidth to probe up for more bandwidth. This
ends up generating the queue but getting more accurate capacity estimates
and identifying great improvements in the capacity, this process only lasts for
the RTT, and it also spends another RTT reducing the pacing rate to 0.75
times the bottleneck bandwidth to eliminate the bottleneck queue piling up
on the buffer and on the link.
• ProbeRTT
    If the new minimum RTT is not evaluated within 10 seconds during
probeBw phase, BBR gets into the probeRTT phase. This phase measures
the actual minimum RTT in the link; therefore, the maximum transmission is
dropped to 4 packets to remove any queue in the bottleneck link. When a new
minimum RTT value is obtained, it is updated as RTprop and maintained for
the next 10 seconds. This phase only lasts for 0.2 seconds.
    Encouraged by BBR’s ever-promising performances, several recent studies
have evaluated BBR on different network setups and application settings. Hock
et al. [15], Ma et al. [16], and Zhang et al. [17] reported severe unfairness issues
in TCP-BBR, specifically when two different TCP flows with varying RTTs
coexist on a common bottleneck link.
    We deployed the BBR algorithm on NS3 simulating environment for test-
ing and analysis purposes based on [17–19]. After a series of scrutinization, we
found that during the probebw phase, the BBR algorithm uses a fixed pacing
gain rate of 1.25 to probe up for more available bandwidth and 0.75 pacing
rate to probe down to eliminate both buffer and link queue generated dur-
ing probe up session. These fixed default pacing rates applied by BBR work
perfectly when only a single TCP flow is used. However, when different TCP
flows with varying RTTs are involved during data transmission, BBR injects
some extra data, which increases the delivery rate capacity during probe up

neck bandwidth, resulting in excessive queue formation (bufferbloat). This is
quite a surprising result because BBR was designed to eliminate bufferbloat
occurrences; this issue was also reported by [20]. Our experimental analysis
also revealed that the 0.75 probe down pacing rate adopted by BBR could

probe up for more bandwidth without considering factors such as bottleneck
buffer queue status or link congestion. When multiple flows with varying RTTs
coexist on a shared bottleneck link, BBR tends to add extra bytes of data,
which will cause an excessive queue, buffer overflow, and link congestion, which
the default probe down pacing rate cannot drain or clear, and this goes down
as the primary contributing factor to the unfairness of the various RTT flows

of long RTT flows, creates a serious network flaw that some users can easily
take advantage of by intentionally inflating the delay (RTTs) on their TCP
flows, intending to create a skewed network throughput to acquire the lion’s
share of the available bandwidth than the rest of other network users.
    Google is upgrading BBR; they have already released BBR version 2.0,
which is in alpha edition [21] to address some of the major concerns observed by
various experimental evaluations [9, 15, 22]. Several evaluations have already
been conducted on the BBR Version 2.0, revealing that the RTT fairness

as provided by the original version of the BBR algorithm, which only func-
tions perfectly when a single TCP flow is used, which is unlikely in our
current networks. BBR-APG was evaluated on the NS3 simulator. Our pro-
posed algorithm’s evaluation analysis performance results showed significant
RTT fairness improvement from the standard BBR algorithm. Our Technique
reported better performance results than other recently published BBR vari-
ants, ensuring various TCP flows with varying RTTs compete more equitably

2 Related Work
After Google published the TCP-BBR, several studies have evaluated its per-
formances under various network setups and conditions [15–17], while others
have tried to improve various aspects of the BBR [8, 25–28].
    According to Mahmud et al. [9], when more than two flows with various
RTTs are involved in data transmission, BBR exhibits substantial throughput
imbalance and packet loss rates. Their research led to the development of the
BBR Advanced (BBR-A) algorithm, which reduces cwnd by half and transmis-
sion rate by 25% when congestion and packet loss are identified. Kim et al. [29]
evaluation of the BBR algorithm reported that long RTT flows consistently
suppress and outperform their short RTT counterparts. This eventually leads
to throughput imbalance and queuing delay as long RTT traffic occupies a
bigger portion of the available bandwidth. To solve the BBR mentioned above
design problems, their study came up with an algorithm that restricts the
inflight cap during probebw phase below 2 BDP when congestion is detected
to minimize and suppress the existing queue; otherwise, it is reset and limited
to 1 BDP. Longer RTT always flows probe for a longer frame time than short
RTTs ones, which makes them dominate the generated queue backlogs, accord-
ing to Ma et al.’s [16] analysis of the BBR algorithm. Therefore, by limiting
and restricting the long duration of the long RTT traffic during the probing
session, their work proposed a strategy that will lower the high dominance of
long RTT flows. Fairness between the long and short RTT flows will result
from this. Through their analysis, Kim et al. [20] found that the BBR typi-
cally sends too much data, leading to unjust bandwidth distribution among
various flows and buffer overflow. As a result, they recommended the Delay-
Aware BBR (DA-BBR) variant to boost throughput and RTT fairness design
issues. Their algorithm regulated the data transmission rate based on the cor-
relation between RTT and RTprop controlling parameters. According to Pan
et al. [30], the majority of the proposed BBR variations algorithms still apply
a fixed cwnd gain of 2 BDP to restrict the amount of data each flow could
transmit. As a result, long RTT flows will always have a larger cwnd com-
pared to short RTT ones. Therefore, they proposed the BBR-ACW variant,
which modifies the cwnd gain adaptively based on the relationship between
the delivery rate and the RTT. According to our earlier research [14], BBR
computed BDP as BltBw * RTprob. Short RTT flows were constrained since

long RTT traffic has a larger RTprop value; hence long RTT flows now occupy
a substantial portion of the available bandwidth. As a result, we introduced
the method BBR-With enhanced RTT fairness (BBR-Efra), which adaptively
modifies the BDP computation values.
    To our knowledge, all published BBR variants blindly apply the fixed pac-

during probe down intervals, respectively, in the probebw phase. The above-

since long RTT traffic has bigger RTprop values; therefore, their cwnd will be
greater than short RTT flows’.

3 Motivation: BBR’s RTT Fairness Issues
From our extensive experimental analysis, we revealed that during probebw
phase, BBR blindly applies a pacing rate of 1.25 to probe up for more avail-
able bandwidth without considering factors like a buffer or even link queue
statuses and sizes, followed by a 0.75 pacing rate to probe down to drain, clear
queue and congestion generated during probe up. Finally, a pacing rate of 1 is
applied for the remaining six RTT cycles to sustain the consistent and stable
bandwidth. When a single TCP flow transverses through the bottleneck link,
BBR converges at the right designed Kleinrock’s optimal operating point, and
in this case, it operates with the maximum expected delivery rate and with
minimal delays. But, when several TCP flows with varying RTTs coexist on a
common bottleneck link, the total data transmission rate usually exceeds the
available bandwidth, resulting in excessive buffer overflows (bufferbloat). The
default probe down pacing rate applied by BBR is not good enough to drain,
clear, and consume the queue generated on the bottleneck buffer and on the
link, resulting in a persistent queue backlog. Due to the excessive transmission
rate and incapacity of releasing the queue, BBR operates at the point which
has shifted slightly to the right of the Kleinrock operating point, as demon-
strated in Figure 3. Based on the queuing theory, bottleneck queue sharing
determines the network flow’s throughput. Based on the queuing theory, the
generated bottleneck queue sharing influences the network flow’s throughput.
When the queue is formed and, with time, becomes persistent, short RTT flows
are prematurely constricted by cwnd, and the flows can not acquire additional
bandwidth even with more frequent probes performed [8].
    Moreover, the long RTT flows have bigger and higher estimated RTprop
values, taking a larger fraction of the already formed persistent queue and
occupying a more significant bandwidth consumption share. The high dom-
inance of long RTT flows makes short RTT ones decrease the delivery rate
of the subsequent bandwidth detection sessions; this makes the transmission
rate of the short RTT flows smaller. In conclusion, when the bottleneck buffer
and the link are overloaded, the longer RTT flows have a higher data-sending

4 Proposed Algorithm: BBR-APG
When several TCP flows coexist on a common shared bottleneck link, BBR
sends extra data and makes the total transmission rate exceed the bottleneck
capacity, forming a queue that, with time, becomes a persistent queue. Based
on our various experimental analyses, bottleneck buffer or link queue statuses
and sizes must be considered before applying probe up and probe down pacing
gain rates during the bandwidth estimation phase. The fixed pacing rate values
offered by BBR and other BBR variants cause the data transferred by the BBR
to surpass the link’s capacity whenever the bottleneck buffer is smaller than
1 BDP. As a result, buffer overflows occur, which leads to persistent queue
creation and problems with fairness amongst flows with various RTTs.
     This study offered a new alternative solution to the TCP-BBR RTT fair-
ness problem, which will substantially enhance it by adaptively regulating the
probebw pacing rates and the upper limit of the cwnd based on different net-
work congestion states using bottleneck inflight queue statuses and shifting the
operating point of the BBR close to the intended operating point and to the
position where the bottleneck buffer is not filled up. Our approach recorded
a substantial throughput increment of short RTT flows that was severely
suppressed.
     Assume there are x number of BBR flows cruising through bottleneck link
with an available bandwidth of B. Let f lowi (i ∈ [1, y]) represent f lowi and
ri (t) express the delivery rate of the f lowi at the time t. Let Ti = qi + pi
represent RTT of f lowi , while qi represents queue delay of f lowi , and pi is

RTprop (obtained in ProbeRTT phase). Let Li = ri ∗(qi +pi ) represent inflight
data of f lowi while Qi = ri ∗ pi ; is the delivery capacity. The bottleneck link’s
queue balance is heavily influenced by qi and ri .
    BBR-APG updates Li and Qi after every probebw RTT cycle to obtain
the latest state of the bottleneck queue status based on different RTT flows.
    Algorithm 1 describes the logic implementation of our proposed algo-
rithm, BBR-APG. When the bottleneck link status becomes Li ≤ 1.25Qi and
when pi > Ti , the bottleneck buffer is either not occupied or very little occupied
with the inflight data, and there is no queue detected on the link, respectively.
Therefore, BBR can probe more available bandwidth with an innovative adap-
tive probe up pacing rate technique instead of the default fixed pacing rate
value of 1.25 on different RTT flows to acquire more available bandwidth,
which is essential during this interval. The adaptive probe up pacing rates
provided by BBR-APG is influenced by inflight queue status; the smaller the
queue size within the buffer, the bigger and larger the pacing rate value to be
assigned and vice versa. In summary, the lesser the inflight queue, the larger
the probe up pacing rate value to transmit more packets, and the bigger the
inflight queue, the smaller the probe up pacing rate value to be assigned to
transmit fewer packets to prevent bufferbloat phenomena. Our algorithm pro-
vided a well-thought-out and crafted equation, which not only perfectly fitted
and provided suitable adaptive probe up pacing values but also values that
ensure short RTT flows are not constricted by cwnd prematurely. But when
the status turns to Li > 1.25Qi and Ti > pi , the network is overloaded; the
bottleneck buffer is experiencing buffer overflows, and the link is congested.
Therefore, BBR-APG can drain and clear the queue generated on the buffer
and the link; our proposed algorithm applies an adaptive probe down pacing
rates instead of the fixed default value of 0.75. BBR-APG determines the val-
ues for its adaptive probe down pacing rate based on the infight queue status.
The bigger and longer the inflight queue, the lower the pacing rate values to be
assigned and vice versa; this technique slows down the sending rate adaptively
and clears the existing queue generated on the bottleneck buffer and on the
link. The adaptive probe down pacing rate values provided by our algorithm is
lower than the default value of 0.75, releasing the queue much quicker than the
original BBR probe down pacing value. From our formulated equation, since
long RTT flows have larger pi , they will experience more Pdown reduction than
short RTT flows, thus guaranteeing improved fairness of the two competing
flows when battling for the available bottleneck bandwidth. Unlike the origi-
nal BBR algorithm, which blindly probes up and probes down using fixed rate
values of 1.25 and 0.75, respectively, without considering factors like a buffer
or link queue statuses, our proposed algorithm considers both buffer and link
queue statuses before adaptively adjusting the pacing rates of both probe up
and probe down sessions based on inflight queue status. Therefore, BBR-APG
eliminates the creation of a persistent queue, increases the fairness between
the competing flows and throughput, and reduces the packet loss rates.

5 Experimental setup and Results analysis

5.1.1 RTT Fairness
We conducted an RTT fairness evaluation of the performances of various
BBR variants with respect to RTT changes. Where flows with varying RTTs
competed for 100 Mbps bandwidth on a 5 BDP bottleneck buffer size. Our
experiments evaluated BBR RTT fairness by comparing 10 ms Vs. 50 ms TCP
RTT flows, as presented in Figure 5.
    Figure 5(a) demonstrates that 10 ms flows hurriedly obtained high band-
width at the startup phase. Once bandwidth normalizes, the average band-
width of 10 ms flows was about 14 Mbps, while the 50 ms flows got an average
bandwidth of 82 Mbps. This shows that BBR short RTT flows were signif-
icantly repressed by long RTT flows. The DA-BBR method demonstrated
better fairness performance results than the traditional BBR algorithm, as
depicted in Figure 5(b). Figure 5(c) BBR-ACW outperformed the standard
BBR and DA-BBR, achieving a throughput differential between the two flows
of 1.4 times. In Figure 5(d), BBR-APG outperforms all its competitors by
achieving a throughput variance of 1.2 when the two different flows com-
pete for the same bottleneck bandwidth. BBR-APG displayed much-desired
aggressiveness and produced better fairness performance than other variants.

6 Conclusion
The primary reasons for the RTT unfairness in the BBR congestion control
algorithm were examined in this work. When two flows with differing RTTs
share and coexist on a single bottleneck link, we noticed a severe unfairness
of allocating bandwidth, where BBR advantages and favors long RTT flows
over short RTT flows. This preference nature of BBR is the primary source of
imbalanced throughput and a serious BBR flaw that some users can easily take
advantage of by inducing delays in their flows to create unwarranted long RTT
flows to unfairly enjoy the lion’s share of the available bandwidth than the
                    Springer Nature 2021 LATEX template


18    BBR-With Adaptive Pacing Gain Rates (BBR-APG)

rest of the users. We suggested the BBR-APG algorithm to address this fun-
damental BBR design problem. Our proposed approach adaptively adjusts the
probebw phase pacing gain rates of each flow based on the inflight queue sta-
tus, unlike the original BBR, which blindly probes up and probes down using
fixed values of 1.25 and 0.75, respectively. With BBR-APG, multiple TCP flow
with different RTT delays can fairly compete for valuable network resources
such as bandwidth. Our proposed technique outperformed other popular BBR
variants in all metrics evaluated in shallow and deeper buffer settings and on
various bottleneck bandwidth setups. In comparison with the BBR algorithm,
our proposed methodology achieved RTT fairness improvements of over 58%,
throughput improvements of over 45% and packet retransmission reductions
of over 52%.
