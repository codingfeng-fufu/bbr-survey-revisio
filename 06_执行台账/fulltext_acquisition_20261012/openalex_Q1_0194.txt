Abstract
Google proposed a new TCP congestion control algorithm based on Bottleneck Bandwidth and Round-trip
propagation time (BBR). BBR achieves high transmission rate and low latency by estimating the available
bottleneck capacity. Compared with the traditional loss-based congestion control algorithms such as CUBIC, BBR
has obvious advantages in throughput. However, some studies have found that BBR has poor fairness among
flows with different Round Trip Times (RTTs), inter-protocol unfairness and high packet loss rate in shallow buffer.
BBR is already used in some cases, so its fairness and practicability improvements become very important. In this
paper, the fairness of BBR is taken as the breakthrough point to deeply analyze the bandwidth probe mechanism.
The pacing rate control model is derived and the effect of pacing gain on BBR performance is revealed.
Furthermore, This paper proposes a BBR-PG algorithm, which adjusts pacing gain based on RTT to improve BBR.
Simulation and real network experiments show that the proposed algorithm improves RTT fairness and inter-
protocol fairness, reduces the retransmission rate,while retaining the throughput advantage of the original BBR.


1. Introduction
In 2016, Google[1] proposed a new congestion control algorithm (CCA) based on Bottleneck Bandwidth and
Round-trip propagation time (BBR). BBR creates a network path model by measuring the available bottleneck
bandwidth (BtlBw) and the Round-Trip propagation time (RTprop) to maximize delivery rate and minimize latency.
The BBR implementation released by Google[2] shows that BBR can significantly improve throughput and
bandwidth utilization compared to loss-based CCAs, such as CUBIC[3]. Due to its excellent performance, BBR has
gained much attention ever since its release. Mathis and Mahdavi[4] claimed that BBR opens a new era in
congestion control. However, some evaluations[5–7] found that the unfairness in sharing bottleneck between BBR
and Reno/CUBIC. Literatures proposed that BBR has the problems of Round Trip Time (RTT) fairness[8–9] and
high packet loss rate in shallow buffer[10–11]. Google is actively addressing these issues and has released the
BBRv2 alpha[12]. Meanwhile, it is also hoped that more researchers will continue to optimize BBR.

BBR has been deployed in some application scenarios, but more research is needed to further improve the BBR to
ensure that there are no more potential vulnerabilities. As a result of this motivation, this paper makes the
following contributions:

  1. The pacing rate control model is established and the origin of BBR fairness issues is revealed. Because the
     RTprop and queuing delay affect the original pacing gain, the RTT fairness and inter-protocol fairness of BBR
     are caused.
  2. A optimization algorithm BBR-PG is proposed, which adaptively adjusts the original fixed pacing gain to
     balance the sending rates of different flows in the bottleneck queue.The phase time of the original BBR is
     optimized to accelerate the flows convergence.
  3. BBR-PG is compared and analyzed by simulation and real network. The results show that BBR-PG maintains
     the throughput advantage of the original BBR, improves RTT fairness and inter-protocol fairness, and reduces
     the retransmission.

The rest of this article is arranged as follows. The Section 2 briefly introduces the principle of BBR and related
optimization research. The theoretical model and derivation of the proposed algorithm are in Section 3. Section 4
is experimental results and analysis. Conclusions will be held in Section 5.

2. Related work
2.1 BBR congestion control
BBR controls congestion by limiting the pacing rate of packets and inflight to one BDP. BBR regards the maximum
bandwidth (delivery rate) of the last 10 RTTs as the current BtlBw, and regards the minimum delay measured in
the past 10 seconds as the current RTprop. The congestion window (CWND) and pacing rate were adjusted by
cwnd_gain and pacing_gain as:

                                     pacing rate=pacing_gain × BtlBw


1

                                         CWND=cwnd_gain × BDP


2

BBR has four control phases: StartUP, Drain, ProbeBW and ProbeRTT. The Phases of BBR is shown in Fig. 1.

In the StartUP phase, the CWND and pacing rate will increase by setting cwnd_gain and pacing_gain as 2/ln2
(about 2.89). If the newly estimated bandwidth of three consecutive RTTs does not increase by at least 25%, BBR
enters the Drain phase. BBR passes the pacing_gain is reduced to ln2/2 (about 0.35) to clear the remaining queue
in the previous phase, cwnd_gain remains unchanged (2/ln2). In the ProbeBW phase, BBR has 8 cycles in the
detection maximum bandwidth (pacing_gain [] = [1.25; 0.75; 1; 1; 1; 1; 1; 1; 1; 1]), and the duration of each
pacing_gain was RTprop. If new RTprop is not sampled again within 10 seconds, BBR enters ProbeRTT. This
phase is used to drain the buffer and obtain the minimum RTT.
2.2. Improvements of BBR
As BBR has gained wide attention, many researchers have evaluated the performance of BBR and conducted
further optimization research to improve its defects. Some researchers have modified control parameters in BBR.
With Tsunami[13] and BBRplus[14] receiving attention, but it is not clear whether either algorithm is applied to the
actual program.

In terms of inter-protocol fairness, Hurtig et al.[15] evaluated BBR's inter-protocol fairness when the traffic
consists of flows of different sizes. Zhang et al.[16] proposed modest BBR,which adjusted the rate according to
the network situation, reduced the retransmission, and realized high throughput and inter-protocol fairness. Ware
et al.[17] provided the first model capturing BBR's behavior in competition with loss-based CCAs. Song et al.[18]
proposed BBR convergence window scaling (BBR-CWS), which adjusted the size of CWND through packet loss
feedback. It reduced excessive retransmission and found a balance between algorithms. Google[19] proposed
BBRv2 and released the preview version (called BBRv2 alpha), which can solve some fairness problems and
limitations of the original BBR. Subsequently, Google[20] proposed a new BBR.Swift algorithm that utilizes the
idea of delay as a congestion signal proposed by Kumar et al.[21] to achieve higher fairness and lower
retransmission. Google continues to update the BBR, which still has RTT fairness and intra-protocol fairness
problems to be further improved. In recent IETF-110[22], Google updated the BBR again. However, the BBR still
has RTT fairness and intra-protocol fairness problems to be further improved.



                                                      Page 3/22
In order to solve the RTT fairness problem, Ma et al. [8] proposed the BBQ algorithm. By setting the upper limit of
the detection time, long RTT flows are prevented from preempting the bandwidth of short RTT flows. When no
persistent queue is detected, BBQ reverts to the original BBR mechanism to maintain bandwidth detection speed.
Yang et al. [23] proposed an Adaptive BBR algorithm, which alleviates the fairness between different RTT flows by
changing the detection period and pacing gain of BBR. Kim et al. [24] proposed a method to suppress the
establishment of bottleneck queues to enhance RTT fairness. Later, they proposed Delay-Aware BBR[25], which
improved the fairness between different RTT flows. In our previous research, we also optimized RTT fairness by
adjusting CWND [26] and sending rate[27] respectively to alleviate RTT unfairness.

BBR has been applied to different network scenarios to improve service quality[28–30]. When BBR is applied,
some users may use these vulnerabilities to compete for bandwidth maliciously. Obviously, new methods are
needed to further improve the fairness and practicability of BBR.

Next, the inter-protocol fairness optimization when BBR and CUBIC coexist is discussed. In addition to the above
pg interference, the cycle of ProbeRTT phase also affects inter-protocol fairness. BBR enters the ProbeRTT phase
at a fixed interval of 10s to remeasure RTprop, due to the existence of CUBIC, the measurement of RTprop will be
inaccurate. When the BBR flows enters the ProbeRTT phase, the active detection of bandwidth by the CUBIC flows
will fill the queue. Since the queue is not empty at this time, this causes the measured RTprop of the BBR flows is
larger than the actual RTprop. Larger RTprop will result in larger CWND, resulting in more inflight, which will benefit
BBR flows. ws are measured with lower RTprop than before. This process is repeated throughout the data
transmission time, and continuous oscillations can lead to unfairness between protocols, regardless of whether
the two flows achieve a fair average throughput.

This problem is solved by randomizing the time to enter the ProbeRTT phase. The fixed interval of 10 seconds in
the original BBR mechanism is changed to a random interval with a mean of 10 seconds as the next time to enter
ProbeRTT phase. The time interval adopted in this paper is shown:

Where Trand is the random interval time, h1 and h2 are set to 5 and 15 respectively, which means the value range is
set from 5s to 15s.

By random intervals, different BBR flows no longer enter the ProbeRTT phase at the same time. Some BBR flows
enter the ProbeRTT phase and measure RTprop through the empty queue. Other BBR/CUBIC flows that are not in
ProbeRTT phase can actively compete for the free bandwidth in 200ms. In this way, BBR-PG sacrifices a small
amount of bandwidth to alleviate aggression and retransmission, thus achieving better fairness.
4. Experiments evaluation
4.1 Testbed setup
This section uses NS3, LAN and WAN networks to evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithm BBR-PG.
the experimental topology is shown in Fig. 4. NS3 is a network emulator that creates a virtual network running
multiple hosts, links, and switches on a single machine. Based on the BBR implementation framework[31], a large
number of simulation experiments have been carried out on NS3, where the condition variables include bottleneck
bandwidth, RTT and buffer size, etc.

In order to eliminate the uncontrollable interference in the network (e.g., background traffic, special token bucket
policy program, AQM deployment, etc.), a LAN network is formed by four Linux hosts, and set flows sharing
bottleneck scenario. NetEm was used to simulate the channel part, and different network environments were
configured.

The WAN experiment was constructed by using 4 Linux physical hosts and 2 cloud servers in the laboratory
(node1 in London, UK and Node2 in Silicon Valley, USA). The bandwidth of each server is 5Mbps, and the latency
is about 300ms and 230ms, respectively. The Linux5.4 kernel module is deployed in the whole cluster, and sch_fq
is loaded to adjust the traffic according to BBR requirements.

Moreover, in order to quantify the fairness under different buffer sizes, Jain fairness index is introduced[32]. Jain
fairness Index can be used to measure fairness in resource competition. The calculation method using Eq. 22,

4.2 Transmission speed
The Web service is set up on the server via Nginx, and 100Mbps files are placed for download. The BBR, BBQ,
BBR-PG and CUBIC were tested in the same time period in each experiment. The experiment is divided into single-
thread and multi-thread tests, and the statistical results are given in Fig. 5. The download speed of BBR、BBQ and
BBR-PG are basically the same, and the three algorithms have a significant effect on improving the transmission
speed. The 5.4 kernel default CUBIC is the slowest. In the experimental process, we found that the speed of BBQ
would increase rapidly after the beginning, and then the speed would decrease significantly, which could be seen
                                                        Page 9/22
in many experiments. On the other hand, BBR and BBR-PG download speeds are relatively stable. At the
beginning, the download speed rose rapidly, then fell back slightly, and finally remained stable. In Fig. 5(b), the

4.3 RTT fairness
The experiment was set up under 0.5BDP and 5BDP buffers, and 10ms RTT flow competed with 50ms RTT flow
for bottleneck bandwidth. Figure 6 shows the throughput comparison results. For BBR and BBQ, when the buffer
increases, the throughput difference between flows increases obviously. Although the throughput of BBR-PG
fluctuates greatly, the throughput difference is reduced. Among the four algorithms, the fairness of BBR-PG is the
best.

Next, the fairness of the four algorithms under different buffer sizes is compared. In 0.1BDP ~ 100BDP buffer,
average throughput and fairness index of 10ms RTT flows and 50ms RTT flows are provided in Fig. 7. The
throughput difference becomes larger with the increase of buffer size. In general, BBR-PG has better RTT fairness
than the other three algorithms, especially in the deep buffer. Compared with BBR, the fairness index increased by
about 53%.

In addition to buffer size has a significant impact on RTT fairness, RTT differences also significantly affect RTT
fairness. As depicted in Figs. 8(a)(b)(c), with the increase of RTT difference, the throughput of long RTT flow
gradually dominates, and the fairness index gradually decreases. The fairness index of BBR is only 0.59. but that
of BBR-PG can be maintained at about 0.95. In Fig. 8(d), the RTT fairness of CUBIC deteriorates with the increase
of RTT ratio, and the final fairness index is about 0.89. On the whole, BBR-PG can maintain high fairness among
different RTT ratios. Especially when the RTT ratio is greater than 4, the fairness index of BBR-PG is the highest,
which improves by 61% compared with BBR.

Then experiments were carried out on LAN. For each test, the number of flows varies from 2 to 4. Figure 9 shows
the statistical results of the throughput ratio of 10ms and 50ms RTT flows.

As observed from the results provided in Fig. 9, 50ms RTT flows have obvious advantages in different buffer
sizes. Although the disadvantage of 10ms RTT flows is alleviated in shallow buffers, their bandwidth share is still
far below its reasonable share. Different from the other three algorithms, 10ms RTT flow of CUBIC is superior in
shallow buffer. Compared with BBR, the fairness of BBR-PG improved by 11%(0.8BDP) and 23%(8BDP)
respectively. This further proves the effectiveness of the simulation results and the proposed algorithm.

Meanwhile, RTT fairness test was carried out in WAN to verify the performance of each algorithm. As we can see
from Fig. 10, the experiment results of WAN were similar to those of NS3 and LAN. The share of throughput
between 50ms RTT flow and 10ms RTT flow in WAN also has a certain deviation. Different from the NS3 and LAN
experiment results, the results in Fig. 10(b) show a different trend from Fig. 9(b). In the deep buffer, the
throughput share of 50ms RTT flow does not increase significantly. The bandwidth sharing bias of two flows in
WAN is less than that of NS3 and LAN experiments.

4.5 Retransmission
The experiments are conducted to verify the influence of different buffer sizes and the number of contended
flows on the retransmission rate. The sender uses different algorithms to transmit single or multiple flows to the
receiver, and the buffer size is set to 0.1BDP or 1BDP. The retransmission rate is shown in Fig. 13. The starting
point is the retransmission rate of a single 10ms RTT flow.

As shown in Fig. 13, the retransmission rate of BBR is significantly higher than that of the other three algorithms.
With the increase of the number of flows, a large amount of retransmission occurs in all four algorithms. The
retransmission rate of BBR-PG is much lower than BBR and BBQ, and slightly 0.5% higher than that of CUBIC. The
reason why the retransmission rate of BBR-PG is higher than CUBIC may be that the aggressive detection
bandwidth brought by pacing gain in BBR-PG results in a certain amount of data packet retransmission, which
needs to be further optimized in future work.

The retransmission rates of the four algorithms were then tested on LAN and WAN networks. The retransmission
rates of the four algorithms are shown in Fig. 14.Similar to Fig. 13, the retransmission rate of BBR is significantly
higher than that of the other three algorithms. When there were 50 flows, the retransmission rate of BBR was
about 12.7%, while the retransmission rate of BBQ and BBR-PG remained around 4.7% and 4.6%. This indicates
that BBR is increasing throughput at the expense of high packet retransmission in shallow buffers. BBR may not
be a good choice if the content passed is sensitive to packet loss. In this case, users need to make a trade-off
between throughput and quality of experience. Overall, the retransmission of BBR-PG was much lower than that of
BBR and 0.4% higher than that of CUBIC. It is proved that BBR-PG can reduce retransmission and improve
performance.


5. Conclusions

                                                      Page 11/22
In this paper, we analyzed and exposed the critical reasons for RTT fairness and inter-protocol fairness problems
in BBR. Then, we established the pacing rate control model to analyze the change of actual pacing gain, and
found that RTT measurement led to the inaccurate evaluation of BtlBw. According to the pacing gain mechanism,
an improved BBR-PG is proposed. BBR-PG adaptively adjusts the pacing rate by increasing the regulator factor γ
instead of the fixed 1.25 or 0.75. Meanwhile, the phase time of ProbeRTT phase is randomized.

Different network test results show that BBR-PG can effectively alleviate the fairness problem in BBR and retain
the advantage of BBR in throughput. In terms of RTT fairness, BBR-PG had the best fairness index. In NS3
simulation network, its fairness index is 50% higher than BBR, and RTT fairness of LAN and WAN networks is also
improved. In the inter-protocol fairness tests, the fairness index of BBR-PG in NS3 simulation network improves by
more than 46% compared with BBR. The LAN network test results also verify the fairness of BBR-PG. The
retransmission test results show that BBR-PG can effectively reduce the retransmission of BBR in shallow buffer.
Overall, BBR-PG not only alleviates the fairness problem of BBR, but also reduces the retransmission.

In the future work, we will continue to optimize the parameters of the BBR-PG algorithm, such as Tinterval and Trand.
We plan to use reinforcement learning to predict network load so that Tinterval or Trand can be adjusted adaptively.
In addition, with the development of BBRv2 version, the improved method can be applied to the latest BBR to
solve its problem.
